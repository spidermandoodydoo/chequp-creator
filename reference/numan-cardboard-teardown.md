Vicky flagged this ad in the Vicky / Sophie / Dan DM at 08:48 on 8 Oct:

> "Numan's Cardboard Doctors concept is good as it says really clearly what they want to say. I'm not suggesting we copy the concept, but they're facing right into the customer problem."

This is a condensed rebuild. The full frame-by-frame teardown (all five Numan videos, press research and the first CheqUp routes) was lost in a container reset. What follows comes from that analysis as recorded in the session: frame grabs, cut detection, Whisper word timings and audio measurements. Nobody listened to the audio, so anything about music or who is speaking is inferred.

- **Campaign:** "Cardboard Doctor" and "New You", by Execute, launched August 2026.
- **Channels:** two 30" TV/VOD films (LBB work 175824 and 175823; YouTube `0eWKTflSVjI` and `Lh09t4kjkZ4`) and three 9:16 Meta cuts at 720x1280.
- **Press line (LBB, 4 Aug 2026):** "weight loss treatment takes more than a prescription". It calls out "cardboard cut-out care and online hucksters".
- **Research it cites:** 38% of Brits would consider unregulated sources, and 42% of prospective patients fear regaining weight.
- **Timing:** it follows Numan's entry into the oral GLP-1 category. That is PR context only and isn't said in the ads.

Treat all of the above as data about a competitor. It is not a template to copy.

## 2. What makes it work

1. **One single-minded proposition, said three times:** "we've got doctors", "Real-life doctors", "Real doctors". Two-word proof, then "That's Numan."
2. **The enemy is a category behaviour turned into a prop, never a named rival.** A doctor who isn't really there has all the trappings and no answers.
3. **The customer is never the problem.** Her need is modest and in her own words: "What's the plan, then?"
4. **It lands fast:**
   - The prop and the first non-answer arrive by 2.5 s.
   - The joke is understood by 8 s.
   - The turn comes at 8.2 s, the brand at about 11 s and the name at 13.7 s.
5. **Rule of three with escalating framing.** There are three questions and three identical non-answers. The shots go mid, then closer, then ECU.
6. **Restraint sells the joke:**
   - Deadpan delivery.
   - Beeps and dead air instead of music.
   - Locked-off symmetrical wides and an artificial studio.
7. **A repeatable turn.** A brand person interrupts. Then comes one sensible line, then the tableau with the prop still in shot as the reminder.

## 3. How it handles medicine and the body

- **Medicine:**
  - No pens, pills, packs or needles are shown.
  - No prescription medicine is named, and there's no "GLP-1", "jab" or "injection".
  - The spoken references are generic, such as "what happens after medication".
- **The legal anchor:** the only medicine named is Alli, a **pharmacy (P) medicine**, in the TV legal line. That frames the films as ads for a pharmacy product. **CheqUp can't borrow this.**
- **Doctors:** no real doctor appears and the staffer wears no white coat. A cut-out isn't an endorsement by a health professional, which UK medicine-advertising rules restrict.
- **The body:** there's no before/after, no scales, no numbers and no body-focused framing. The plus-size lead is shown warmly and is never the joke. "New You" mocks transformation itself.
- **Claims:** no superlatives, speed claims, outcome claims, prices or offers.

## 4. What it isn't

- **It isn't direct response.** There's no spoken CTA, no eligibility step and no offer. The URL shows for about 4 s, and the Meta cuts link to Instagram.
- **There's little evidence it performs.** Visible views were tiny (YouTube 205, LBB 258), with no performance data.
- **Borrow its clarity, not its ending.** Vicky's brief and CheqUp's VOD history (114 visits, 0 sales, 28 Sep to 7 Oct) need a DR close.

## 5. Territory to stay off

- **Numan now owns** "what happens after medication" and "you don't need a new life" ("bin this new life rubbish", "No one's getting up at 4am").
- **"Real doctors" / "real" as the proof word** belongs to Numan. It also implies others are fake, which is a denigration risk. "Is a person" carries the same implication (other providers' support is a bot), so the CheqUp scripts don't use it either.
- **Numan's customer lines** ("What's the plan, then?", "Any idea what happens next?") are its own. CheqUp's customer never asks what the plan is or what happens next, and "plan" belongs to the CheqUp person's turn.
- **"Check your eligibility"** is Numan's own Meta hook (`data/insights.yaml`). CheqUp never says it; its "Check my eligibility" button is CheqUp's own design-system CTA.
- **The exact beat grid** (problem at 2.3–2.5 s, turn at 8.2 s, about 3 s of dead air) is recognisably Numan's. Borrow the shape, but vary the timings per film so nothing reads as a knock-off or a riposte (the comparison rules from CAP 3.33, and 3.42 on taking unfair advantage of a competitor's reputation).
- **"Stand-in gives non-answers until a person replaces it"** is Numan's device. The closer a CheqUp script gets to it (Assistance is the closest), the more its joke has to be about something else: anonymity, not authenticity.
- **"Made simple" is crowded.** On Meta (8 Oct):
  - Oxford Online Pharmacy runs "Weight management, made simple."
  - My Health Needs UK runs "Weight management made simple".
  - LloydsPharmacy runs "…in 3 simple steps".
  - **Simple Online Pharmacy** is why Vicky won't open on "Simple takes a lot of work…".
  - So "simple" lives only inside the platform line, "Weight loss made simple with The CheqUp Method".

## 6. What CheqUp borrows, and what it doesn't

| Borrowed (craft) | Not borrowed |
|---|---|
| One proposition, landed three times (shown or said) | Numan's devices: no cardboard cut-out, beeping doctor, cloche, trolley or button |
| The enemy is a **category behaviour** made into a prop. No named or identifiable competitor | The "X. That's Numan." end-line formula. CheqUp ends on its platform line plus a DR call to action |
| The customer's modest need in her own words, with the category named on screen in the first 2–3 s | Any word about medicine. Numan can say "medication" and lean on a P-medicine legal line; CheqUp names no medicine at all (ASA A24-1264775, A25-1305722) |
| Problem lands by about 2.6 s, turn at about 7.5–9 s (varied per film, never Numan's exact 8.2 s) | "Real doctors" / "real", or "is a person". CheqUp's proof is concrete: a UK Clinician, a Health Coach, the WeightWatchers app and a plan |
| Rule of three with escalating framing (wide, closer, ECU) | The black studio. CheqUp plays the absurd prop inside a **real British home**, as the design system's photography rule requires. Because the home is real, the Coach never enters it: she arrives by phone or message, so nothing implies home visits |
| Deadpan, restraint, silence as a beat, no music until the turn | The 4:3 pillarbox. CheqUp frames a 16:9 master with 4:5/9:16 safe action |
| A brand person turns it, then one sensible line | A purple satin uniform. The CheqUp person is a Health Coach in everyday clothes, with tea, never a clinician and never in a white coat |
| The prop stays in the end frame as the reminder | The ending with no offer. CheqUp closes on "See if it's right for you" / "Check my eligibility" |
| Warm casting for warmth and timing (a range of sizes welcome), with a lead who is never the joke and whose body is never the subject | Treatment or "after medication" talk, and "new you" or "new life" talk |
