# Three Numan-style scripts for CheqUp (8 Oct 2026, revised after review)

This answers Dan's note that the earlier type-card routes were "nothing like the numan example", and Vicky's brief (`reference/creative-direction-2026-10-08.md`):
- Be braver and less subtle.
- Face the customer problem as directly as Numan's "Cardboard Doctors" does, without copying it.
- Use the platform line "Weight loss made simple with The CheqUp Method".
- End on a direct-response close.
- Never open on "Simple takes a lot of work…".

The teardown, and what we borrow and don't, is in `reference/numan-cardboard-teardown.md`.

Each script has two forms:
- **(A) A live-action shooting script** for a production company: 30" master plus a 15" cut-down.
- **(B) A no-face motion board** that renders in `cqf`: a synthetic customer voice and an announcer, sound effects and chat bubbles, with AI b-roll showing hands, objects and rooms only.

**Revision 2 (after a compliance and craft review, 8 Oct).** In short:
- **Jo never walks into Sue's home.** She arrives the way the service works, by phone or message, and every film says "by message or call".
- **Assistance escalates on anonymity, not time.** "Passed to the team." becomes "The team.", then "Team." It turns on a name, and the proposition is "Your Health Coach has a name." The kiosk, the beacon, the hold music and the word "assistance" are gone.
- **Good luck** no longer echoes Numan's questions. The cards name the category, and the default proposition is "A plan, made with you."
- **Standard plan:** the letterhead names the category, the first letter says "To the occupier," instead of "Dear Gary,", and Jo asks "Susan or Sue?".
- **Every film is re-timed from its dialogue.** The turns no longer copy Numan's 8.2 s.
- **"Eligibility" is never spoken.** It stays on the button.
- **Motion boards:**
  - The Coach is never a synthetic voice.
  - The enemy looks off-brand, and the Coach chat brands the turn.
  - The dead air sits on the third non-answer.
  - No role tag sits over a person.
  - There are no fallback stills, and 16x9 is dropped.
- **New legal questions on every board:** comparative claims, Coach contact and an indirect-POM read. BCAP checks come before Clearcast.

## Campaign rules (all three scripts)

### Structure
This is Numan's shape, not Numan's timings. Each film lands its beats at different times, so none copies Numan's grid (problem at 2.3 s, turn at 8.2 s, 3 s of dead air). The 15" versions keep all three non-answers, faster, with the turn at about 5–6 s.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–2.6 s | Her modest need, in her own words. The category is named on screen by her line, a card or a letterhead. Then the prop's first non-answer |
| 2.6–8 s | Two more non-answers, each shorter than the last, framed tighter each time: mid, then closer, then ECU. Her lines shrink too. No music |
| 7.5–9 s | The turn. Jo arrives the way the service really works, by phone or message. Never in person |
| 14–21 s | The proposition as a super, held in about 2 s of room tone, with the prop still in shot |
| 16–25 s | One sensible line: the Method, concretely. Health Coach, UK Clinician, WeightWatchers app, plan |
| 24–30 s | End frame, with the prop still in the corner (see "Direct-response close") |

**Timing method:**
- Every shot is timed from its dialogue at 2.9 words/s. "chequp.com" counts as three spoken words ("check up dot com").
- Add 0.3 s per beep, buzz or clatter, and 0.4 s per hesitation.
- Every shot below holds its lines with time to spare, and each shot list adds up to exactly 30.0 s or 15.0 s.
- The end-frame VO needs about 4.5 s.

### Recurring cast
The same two characters appear in all three films, so the campaign builds a world, as Numan's did.

**Sue, the customer**
- Woman, 45–55 (the brief allows 35–60), any ethnicity.
- Warm, quick, with great deadpan timing. She is the smartest person in every scene.
- **Casting brief:** cast for warmth and timing; a range of sizes welcome. Size isn't the casting criterion. A25-1305722 upheld that a CheqUp ad presented a larger body as undesirable.
- **Guardrails for the director:**
  - She is never the joke, and her body is never the subject.
  - Eye-level or slightly low angles, which read as dignified.
  - Her own everyday wardrobe (cardigan, jeans, slippers at home), and no change in her appearance during the film.
  - Her only words about weight are her own modest line. That line lands on a close-up, and it's never intercut with framing that shows off her body.
- Must clearly read 25+ and not underweight (CAP 13.3).

**Jo, the CheqUp Health Coach**
- 30s–50s, any gender (scripted as a woman). Warm, unhurried, dry.
- Everyday clothes in a lavender knit (CheqUp's primary light). It is not a uniform.
- **Remote, as the service is.** Jo is never in Sue's home. She calls or messages. We see her at her own desk in a bright, plain room: a window, a notebook, a CheqUp mug and her phone on speaker. It's not a clinic, and there's no call-centre headset.
- **Brand first on screen:** the caller ID or message sender, "Jo · CheqUp Health Coach", at the turn. Then her CheqUp mug.
- **Never** a white coat, stethoscope, scrubs or lanyard. Never presented as a clinician, and never says anything about medicine.
- The UK Clinician is mentioned, never shown. This avoids health-professional endorsement issues and the white-coat trope Numan mocks.

### Look and sound
- **Sue's location:** one real Victorian or Edwardian terraced house (kitchen, hallway, stairs), shot naturally bright.
  - This is where we differ from Numan's black studio: the absurd prop intrudes on a real British home. That follows CheqUp's design-system rule of real homes and natural light.
  - Because the home is real, the Coach must never be physically in it. Otherwise viewers could reasonably infer home visits or in-person coaching.
- **Jo's location:** a second small set for her desk, on the same day if possible. Lock off both sides with matching eyelines for the split-screen tableaux.
- **Camera:** locked-off, symmetrical wides, slightly low. Shoot a 16:9 master with 4:5 and 9:16 safe action in the centre. No 4:3 pillarbox.
- **Music:** none until the turn. The warm piano bed enters with the sensible line, with one soft note on the button.
- **Sound:**
  - The comedy is dialogue, room tone and the prop's own sound: beep, tinny tune, letterbox clatter, phone.
  - Dead air is real dead air: record a clean room-tone pass on every set-up.
- **Loudness:** -23 LUFS for VOD and TV (Numan delivered -22.9), and -14 LUFS for social, as the `cqf` renderer does.

### Legal lines
**Live action:** "Actor portrayal. Illustrative scenario. Health Coach support by message or call. CheqUp Health Ltd · Regulated by the CQC and GPhC · Eligibility criteria apply."

**Motion boards:** "Illustrative scenarios. CheqUp Health Ltd · Regulated by the CQC and GPhC · Eligibility criteria apply."
- No photo of a real person can appear, because there are no fallback stills.
- If a real photo is ever added, add "Actor portrayal."
- Whether GPhC must appear in these lines is an open legal question.

### Compliance spine
Two upheld ASA rulings apply: A24-1264775 and A25-1305722.

- **No prescription medicine, named or implied:**
  - No word, image or prop that names or implies one: no prescription, jab, pen, injection, pill, tablet, oral, dose, GLP-1, "medication" or "treatment".
  - No parcel, box, bag or delivery that could read as medicine. The arm in Good luck hands over greetings cards and nothing else.
- **No speed, efficacy or superlative claims, implied ones included:**
  - No non-answer built on waiting ("shortly", hold music).
  - No line that implies success becomes less a matter of luck.
- **No body-image negativity.**
- **No identifiable competitor:**
  - Each prop is a generic category behaviour. No retail lookalike, such as a self-checkout "assistance" beacon.
  - No lookalike UI, livery, tone of voice or slogan from any provider.
  - Nothing echoes Numan's devices, its "real doctors" proof or its lines ("What's the plan, then?", "Any idea what happens next?").
  - Nothing echoes Numan's Meta hook "Check your eligibility".
- **No testimonials, and no AI person or AI voice presented as a member, coach or clinician.** No role tag sits over a person in the motion boards.
- **The service is shown as it works:** remote, by message or call.

### Open legal questions (in every board's `approvals_pending`)
1. **Comparative or category claims** (CAP 3.1, 3.3, 3.7; 3.33 if a rival becomes identifiable). Each device implies the category behaves worse:
   - Others' support is anonymous.
   - Others sign you up and leave you to it.
   - Others' "personal" plans are generic.

   Before publication legal needs:
   - (a) Evidence that the parodied behaviour is a real, typical category practice. Voy, Juniper and Numan all market personalised plans or programmes.
   - (b) Evidence of CheqUp's own journey: a human Coach for every member, plans individually built, and no auto-reply in the channels a member uses (site chat, onboarding emails, app nudges and the bundled WeightWatchers app). If any of these is automated, the ads mislead about CheqUp itself.
2. **Coach contact.** This is a question, not a conclusion.
   - Does any film still imply a reply time or availability? If legal reads one, CheqUp must hold evidence of typical first-contact and reply times (CAP 3.7; A24-1264775 was a speed claim).
   - Confirm exactly which contact modes exist: message, call, or video too.
3. **Indirect POM read.** Several signals together can read as a prescribing pharmacy service:
   - the online sign-up;
   - "a UK Clinician";
   - "Eligibility criteria apply";
   - "Regulated by the CQC and GPhC";
   - the "Check my eligibility" button.

   ASA guidance says a consultation ad should not indicate that the likely outcome is a POM, and A25-1305722 upheld POM promotion without a named medicine. "Eligibility" is no longer spoken. Legal should say whether GPhC must appear, and confirm a non-medication membership path exists that makes "eligibility" accurate.
4. **Each proposition needs substantiation.** Assistance needs one named Coach per member. Standard plan needs individually built plans.
5. **"A UK Clinician reviews your history."** Confirm a clinician reviews each member's history. The motion boards no longer say members talk to the clinician, or that the clinician "knows" them.
6. **WeightWatchers:**
   - Partner approval.
   - Confirm "included" is true for every membership tier.
   - Give WeightWatchers sight of the full scripts, because its app sits next to an anti-automation joke.
7. **The landing page.** A legal-approved page, never /how-it-works.

### Broadcast (VOD and TV): check before any Clearcast submission
Clearcast typically applies BCAP-level standards even on VOD, including the weight-control section. These points come from the review; confirm the current wording with Clearcast.
- **BCAP 12.3:** ads for remote personalised weight-control advice are acceptable only if everyone giving that advice is suitably qualified and regulated by a recognised professional body.
  - The Health Coach planning by message or call is exactly that.
  - Confirm the Coaches' qualifications and regulator, for example an HCPC-registered dietitian or an AfN-registered nutritionist.
  - If they aren't regulated, keep the live-action off TV and VOD.
- **BCAP 12.15:** a weight-control service must make clear that dietary control is necessary.
  - Legal to draft a dietary-control super for the broadcast cut.
  - Avoid SmPC-style wording such as "adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity", which itself implies a POM.
- **BCAP 12.11:** a dramatised case with a lead who may seem obese is acceptable only under 12.11.1 (medically supervised treatment). That pulls the ad towards the POM framing. Agree the casting position with Clearcast early.
- Clearcast pre-clearance before any VOD or TV use, and legal sign-off on every item above.

### Direct-response close
Every film ends on the DR close, with the prop still in the corner:

| | Voiceover | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| **Live action** | Jo: "Weight loss made simple, with The CheqUp Method, at chequp.com." | Supers: the platform line, "See if it's right for you", a "Check my eligibility" button, chequp.com and the legal line |
| **Motion, 30"** | The platform-line card, then "See if it's right for you at chequp.com." | Endcard headline "See if it's right for you.", a "Check my eligibility" button and the legal line |
| **Motion, 15"** | The platform line | Endcard headline is the platform line, plus the button and the legal line |

**Why "eligibility" isn't spoken:**
- It stays on the button, which is CheqUp's own design-system CTA.
- "Check your eligibility" is Numan's Meta hook (`data/insights.yaml`).
- Said and shown three times next to "a UK Clinician" and "Regulated by the CQC and GPhC", it reads as a prescribing service.

The landing page is still TBC: legal must approve one that isn't /how-it-works.

## 1. Assistance

**Logline:** Sue asks the little answering machine on her kitchen worktop for some help with her weight. It passes her to "the team". Which team? "The team." Who? "Team." Her phone rings. It's Jo, her CheqUp Health Coach.

**Proposition:** *Your Health Coach has a name.* It lands three times:
1. The machine's nameless "team", shrinking to "Team."
2. "It's Jo."
3. The super over the room tone.

**The enemy (category behaviour):** support that never tells you who you're talking to, such as an auto-reply that passes you to an anonymous "team".
- It's about anonymity, not waiting. No line mentions time, there's no hold music, and nobody says "someone will be with you".
- The machine stands in for a support channel, not a professional.

**The risk:** this is still the script closest to Numan's shape, where a stand-in gives non-answers until a person replaces it. That's why it runs third.

### Cast
Sue and Jo (see "Recurring cast"), plus the **machine voice**:
- A bright, flat, synthetic female voice.
- Either a VO artist reading flat, or a licensed TTS voice. A synthetic voice is fine for a machine; the Coach is always a human actor.
- It must not imitate any real brand's system voice.

### Set and props
- **Sue's kitchen:** a worktop, a kettle, a fridge, a small table and a fruit bowl.
- **The answering machine** (art department):
  - A generic, unbranded 1990s-style answering machine: beige plastic, one red light and a small speaker grille.
  - No logo, beacon or screen, and nothing that recalls a retailer's self-checkout, a bank or any provider.
  - Nobody says "assistance".
- **Sue's mobile,** face-up on the table. A practical screen shows an incoming call from "Jo · CheqUp Health Coach"; legal signs off the graphic.
- **Jo's desk:** a window, a notebook and pencil, a CheqUp mug, and her phone on speaker.
- **Small props:** Sue's tea towel (over her shoulder from shot 1; it pays off at the end) and her mug.

### Why it works (Numan's craft)
- One idea, repeated: a named person, not a nameless team.
- The enemy is something a whole category does: support that never says who you're talking to. It is never a rival anyone could name.
- Her need is modest and in her own words.
- The machine's answers shrink as the framing tightens: "Passed to the team.", then "The team.", then "Team." Her questions shrink too: seven words, then two, then one.
- The turn is a name. Then 2 s of dead air sit on the machine, and the machine stays in the end frame.

### How it differs from Numan
- There is no studio, cut-out, doctor-shaped prop, cloche, button or "real".
- The stand-in is a support channel, not a professional. The proposition is about knowing who you're talking to, not about authenticity.
- Jo never enters. She calls, as she would.
- The timings differ: the problem lands at 2.6 s, the turn at 8.8 s, and the dead air is about 2 s.
- It ends on a DR close.

## 2. Good luck

**Logline:** Sue signs up, closes her laptop and asks where she starts.
1. An anonymous arm hands her a card: "Good luck with the weight loss x".
2. Then a bigger, musical one: "From all of us x".
3. Then a "Good luck" balloon.

Her phone buzzes. It's a message from Jo, her Health Coach: "Shall we make a plan?"

**Proposition:** *A plan, made with you.* It lands three times:
1. Her questions are met with "good luck".
2. "Shall we make a plan?" The proposition word belongs to CheqUp, not to her question.
3. The super over the room tone.

**Legal option:** "Less luck. More plan." can be used only if legal approves it. It can read as an outcome claim (success less a matter of chance; CAP 13.1, 13.8, 3.7), and "less luck" read cold means less good fortune.

**The enemy (category behaviour):** being signed up and sent off with good wishes and nothing else.
- It becomes greetings-card culture: a leaving-do card, a musical card, a foil balloon.
- It's very British, and it hurts no one.

### Cast
Sue and Jo, plus a **hand model**: one arm in a plain grey suit sleeve, never seen above the elbow.

### Set and props
- **Location:** Sue's kitchen table, with a laptop, a mug, and her phone face-down.
- **Props:**
  - A small card in gold foil script: **"Good luck with the weight loss x"**. It puts the category on screen by about 2.2 s, and the words are the card's, not hers.
  - An oversized musical card: **"GOOD LUCK with the weight loss. From all of us x"**, with dozens of strangers' signatures. It plays a tinny, original, cheap-electronic tune.
  - A silver foil "Good luck" balloon on a curling ribbon, tied to Sue's chair. Have three, for takes.
  - Jo's desk: a notebook and pencil, a CheqUp mug and her phone.
- **The arm hands over cards and nothing else.** No parcels, boxes, bags or envelopes: nothing that could read as a medicine delivery.

## 3. Standard plan

**Logline:** Sue's "personal weight loss plan" drops through the letterbox: "To the occupier,". She's Sue. The next one says "Dear valued customer,". Still Sue. The next, "Dear {first_name},". Her phone rings. It's Jo, her Health Coach: "Susan or Sue?"

**Proposition:** *A plan with your name on it.* It lands three times:
1. Three letters that don't know her name.
2. "Susan or Sue?" / "Hi, Sue."
3. The super over the room tone. In the motion board, it's a CheqUp card that reads "Dear Sue,".

**The enemy (category behaviour):** one-size plans with a mail-merge "personal" touch. The letters are addressed to nobody (the occupier), then to a category (the valued customer), then to a blank field (`{first_name}`).

**Why not "Dear Gary,":** a plan addressed to another person would imply that rivals send personal health plans to the wrong person. That's a data-handling allegation, not a joke about sameness.

## Motion boards: how they're built, and what to check on the PC

### Voices
- **Roles:**
  - `customer` uses Chatterbox with `customer_ref.wav`, with Kokoro bf_isabella as the fallback.
  - `announcer` reads every spoken CheqUp line, in the third person.
- **The Health Coach is never a synthetic voice.**
  - Her chat bubbles are unvoiced, like the bot bubbles.
  - An AI voice reading her first-person lines would be an AI person presented as a coach, against the preset never-rule. If found out, it would undercut "has a name".
  - First-person Coach dialogue lives in the live action, read by an actor under "Actor portrayal".
- **Automated replies are unvoiced** (a chime and a bubble). That's the equivalent of Numan's beeps.
- **The customer's voice is synthetic**, and no real person is cloned. She is a dramatised prospective customer, not a member or a testimonial (`approvals_pending.voices`).
- **Hesitations are two VO lines with an explicit gap** (`pause_after: 0`, then `pause_before` of 0.5–0.6). The preset's `tighten_pauses` (0.22 s down to 0.15 s) applies to the customer too, and would otherwise crush the beat.
- **The platform line** is read in the preset's `tagline` style, slower after a beat.

### Timing
- **Lengths at the 2.9 words/s estimate:**
  - assistance-30: 28.8 s.
  - good-luck-30: 28.1 s.
  - standard-plan-30: 29.5 s.
  - The 15" boards: 14.2–14.6 s.
- **The real read changes nothing.** That's with "chequp.com" spoken as three words and the tagline at tempo 0.8: the platform card is 4.5 s and the 30" endcard 4.3 s for exactly that reason.
- **At a slow 2.6 words/s** the boards run 29.6, 28.7 and 30.4 s, and the 15" boards 14.2–14.9 s.
- **Bubble timing:**
  - Every bot or Coach bubble lands at least 0.45 s after the estimated end of the line before it.
  - Unvoiced bubbles use fixed `at` times. **Re-set them after the PC voice pass** if a read runs slow, because the renderer has no `after_line` yet.

### Sound
- **SFX kinds:**
  - `chime` for automated replies.
  - `ding` for Coach bubbles (a message arriving).
  - `hold_music` only in Good luck, as the musical card's tinny tune.
  - No SFX on the letters.
- **Music:** `silence` windows cover every scene before the turn, and the warm bed enters with the Coach chat.
  - There are no other mutes, because sub-0.5 s mutes read as dropouts.
  - The dead air is the silent hold on the third non-answer.
